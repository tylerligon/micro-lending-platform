### 2. Business Problem and Project Purpose

The project models a small community lending service where members request modest loans and other members contribute to funding them. If requests, lender promises, and repayments are tracked in separate spreadsheets or messages, the service cannot reliably tell whether a request has been approved, whether enough funding has been committed, or which scheduled amounts remain unpaid. Multiple lenders may promise the same remaining amount, and a repeated repayment submission may incorrectly reduce a balance twice. Reviewers also need to distinguish an overdue payment from a loan that has been fully repaid.

The platform will provide a shared record of each request, its assessment and review decision, lender participation, the resulting loan, and its repayment history. Borrowers will see their obligations and record simulated repayments. Lenders will inspect approved requests and monitor loans they fund. Administrators will review requests and follow up on delinquency, while auditors will reconstruct relevant actions without changing them.

The database is the central project deliverable. A basic web interface and supporting services will demonstrate the workflows and authorization rules. All monetary activity is fictional: funding and repayment records represent classroom business events, with no transfer of real money.

### 3. Scope

| In Scope | Out of Scope |
| --- | --- |
| Administrator-created sample accounts, sign-in, business-role assignments, and account restrictions | Public signup, identity-verification vendors, SSO, MFA, and account-recovery services |
| Borrower profiles, multiple concurrent requests and outstanding loans, and retained assessment inputs | Credit-bureau integration or collection of real identity, bank, or payment-card information |
| Automated eligibility assessment under a documented demonstration policy and an administrator's subsequent approval or rejection | Machine-learning training, external credit scores, risk-based pricing, or production underwriting |
| One or more lenders contributing simulated commitments, including additional contributions to the same approved open request | Wallets, lender liquidity checks, bank disbursements, payment processors, or currency conversion |
| Full-funding activation, repayment schedules, partial and early simulated repayments, balances, and delinquency | Interest, fees, penalties, lender payouts, investment returns, refinancing, debt collection, and loan write-offs |
| Request cancellation/expiration and release of commitments before activation | Individual commitment withdrawal or amendment, contributions after full funding, or partial loan activation |
| Administrator review notes and participant-visible borrower identifiers, repayment schedules, and delinquency | Peer voting, public ratings, messaging, email, SMS, and push notifications |
| Role and ownership restrictions, protected financial history, and audit reports | Production banking compliance, enterprise fraud detection, high availability, or disaster-recovery infrastructure |
| Operational and aggregate reports, normalized data, reproducible builds, and database performance demonstrations | A separate warehouse, distributed data platform, or mandatory application cache |
| Basic web workflows and backend extraction/update services | Mobile applications and production hosting |

The release preserves finalized financial records. Correction before activation uses draft editing or cancellation followed by a new request. After activation, an administrator may append an explanatory note; monetary reversals and schedule renegotiation are outside this release. No role may silently edit a posted repayment to correct it.

### 4. Stakeholders and User Roles

| Stakeholder / Role | Responsibilities | Database Needs | Typical Access |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Borrower | Maintain a private profile, request a loan, inspect its progress, and record simulated repayments | Own profile, requests, decisions, schedules, balances, and receipts | Create/edit own drafts; submit/cancel own eligible requests; repay own outstanding loans |
| Lender | Inspect eligible requests, make initial or additional funding commitments, and monitor funded loans and late payments | Limited request listings, own commitments, and borrower identifiers, schedules, balances, and overdue details for associated loans | Read permitted listings and participation-scoped loan/delinquency reports; create own commitments |
| Administrator | Maintain sample users and role assignments, review eligible requests, restrict accounts, and document follow-up | Review queue, private assessment context, operational records, exception reports, and audit history | Controlled administrative actions; no direct changes to finalized financial records |
| Auditor | Independently inspect business activity and access controls | Read-only operational summaries, entitlement history, and redacted audit history | Read within the project dataset; no approval or transaction authority |
| System operations | Run automated assessment, expiration, and delinquency evaluation | Submitted requests, fixed policy D1, funding deadlines, and loan obligations | Technical service identity limited to those operations; not an assignable human business role |

A user may be both Borrower and Lender, but cannot fund their own request. In this release, Administrator and Auditor accounts do not also hold Borrower or Lender roles, and Administrator and Auditor are separate roles. This keeps the demonstration's oversight independent of participation.

Business roles describe application permissions. They are distinct from MySQL accounts and grants. Users sign in to the application; they do not receive database credentials. Technical accounts and their privileges will be specified in the TDD. Server-side authorization must establish the acting user and allowed records independently of browser-supplied role or owner values.

### 6. Transaction Flows / Use Cases

A use case may span several user sessions. It is not one long database transaction. Only the explicitly identified action boundaries must succeed or fail together; no transaction remains open while waiting for a reviewer or another lender.

#### UC-01 - Loan Request, Assessment, and Funding

| Item | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Goal | Convert an eligible, reviewed request into a fully funded simulated loan with an agreed repayment schedule. |
| Primary actor | Borrower; Administrator and one or more Lenders participate. |
| Trigger | A Borrower needs a small loan and submits a completed request. |
| Preconditions | Borrower is active, holds the Borrower role, and has complete assessment inputs. Other requests and outstanding loans are allowed and are included in the combined-exposure assessment. Participating accounts and fixed D1 configuration exist. |
| Related requirements | FR-05 through FR-16; FR-27 through FR-32. |
| Main success flow | 1. Borrower creates and checks a Draft request. 2. Submission freezes its terms, combined-exposure and delinquency snapshot, and policy label D1 under the borrower-level concurrency rule. 3. Automated assessment stores its result and reasons. A Pass moves the request to PendingReview. 4. An Administrator rechecks current borrower eligibility, records approval, and opens a funding window. 5. Lenders inspect the limited listing and may submit initial or additional commitments, each with a new operation reference. 6. Each commitment is checked against ownership, account status, borrower eligibility, request state, deadline, operation-reference reuse, and remaining capacity. 7. A nonfinal commitment is retained as Pending. 8. The commitment reaching the full principal creates the loan and schedule, changes all commitments to Applied, and marks the request Funded. 9. The relevant success audit accompanies each committed action. |
| Alternate/exception flows | Missing or out-of-range inputs leave the Draft unchanged. Assessment Fail leads to Rejected with reasons. A processing failure leaves Submitted unchanged and creates no assessment result; it may be retried. Administrator rejection ends review. Unauthorized, self-funding, conflicting-reference, over-capacity, and late commitments are rejected; repeat participation itself is permitted. An identical retry returns the original receipt. Failed current affordability or delinquency checks block approval/new funding without changing the retained assessment; the request remains in its prior state for a later valid attempt, cancellation, or expiration. Cancellation or expiration releases pending commitments. Failure during final funding rolls back the new commitment and activation work; earlier committed pledges remain Pending and the request remains OpenFunding. |
| Postconditions | Exactly one Active loan exists for a funded request; Applied commitments and scheduled principal both total the requested principal. Otherwise no loan exists and the request retains its valid current or terminal state. |

**Action boundaries:** submission and its snapshot/exposure reservation/audit; assessment result and its state/audit; review decision and its state/deadline/audit; each commitment and its audit; and final funding plus every activation record. Cancellation and expiration each change the request, release its pending commitments, and record the audit together.

#### UC-02 - Repayment Processing and Loan Completion

| Item | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Goal | Record one simulated repayment and show the correct remaining obligations without duplicate credit or partial records. |
| Primary actor | Owning Borrower. |
| Trigger | Borrower selects one owned LoanID and submits an amount and a repayment operation reference. |
| Preconditions | Borrower can sign in, holds the Borrower role, owns an Active loan, and has positive outstanding principal. Restricted borrowers remain able to repay. |
| Related requirements | FR-17 through FR-22; FR-27 through FR-32. |
| Main success flow | 1. Authenticate the actor and verify ownership of the selected loan. 2. Resolve an exact retry before applying a new financial effect. 3. Validate the current loan state and amount. 4. Record the repayment with the server's posting time. 5. Allocate it in installment due order, carrying any excess to later unpaid installments. 6. Calculate the resulting installment and loan balances and current delinquency. 7. If principal is fully repaid, mark the loan Repaid and retain completion time. 8. Retain the success audit and return the receipt and updated summary. |
| Alternate/exception flows | Reject another user's loan, an inactive user, unsupported amounts, overpayment, or a new payment on a Repaid loan. An exact authorized retry returns the earlier receipt even if that repayment completed the loan. Reuse of its reference with a different amount or loan is rejected. If any repayment, allocation, state, or audit write fails, all effects of that new repayment are rolled back. |
| Postconditions | Repayment allocations total the posted amount on the selected loan only; other loans of the same Borrower are unchanged. Outstanding principal and installment remainders agree. The original schedule is unchanged, and the audit identifies the actor and financial effect. |

**Action boundary:** the repayment, every allocation, loan completion/observation changes, and associated success audit form one atomic transaction. Previously committed payments are not undone by failure of a later payment.

#### UC-03 - Delinquency Evaluation, Lender Monitoring, and Administrative Follow-up

| Item | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Goal | Identify past-due obligations and record accountable follow-up. |
| Primary actor | Administrator; participating Lenders inspect permitted results, and routine evaluation runs under the service identity. |
| Trigger | Daily evaluation, an explicit evaluation request, or a repayment changes the observed delinquency condition. |
| Preconditions | Loans and obligations exist; the evaluation uses an explicit server-controlled time. |
| Related requirements | FR-03, FR-19, FR-22, FR-23, FR-26, FR-28. |
| Main success flow | 1. Evaluate every outstanding loan independently at a stated time, including multiple loans of one Borrower. 2. Identify unpaid amounts whose due date has passed. 3. Record the latest observation and audit any change in delinquency. 4. Show the Administrator and each participating Lender the permitted BorrowerID, LoanID, repayment schedule, overdue amount, oldest overdue due date, and days past due; the Lender sees only loans they funded. 5. Administrator appends a follow-up note and may restrict the account through FR-03; independently, BR-07 blocks new credit approval/funding while any of that Borrower's outstanding loans is delinquent. 6. A later repayment clears delinquency on the paid loan only when no overdue amount remains; other delinquent loans continue to block new credit. Full settlement completes only that loan. Clearing all delinquency does not automatically remove an Administrator-imposed restriction. |
| Alternate/exception flows | A fully paid installment or one due today is not overdue. Re-running evaluation without a condition change creates no duplicate delinquency-transition event. A failed per-loan observation update rolls back with its audit; other loans already evaluated remain valid. An unauthorized user cannot run administrative evaluation or add a note. |
| Postconditions | Reports show obligations as of their stated time. Observation history distinguishes when delinquency became effective from when it was detected. Follow-up never changes the original financial record. |

M:N coverage. UserRole retains role-membership episodes. FundingCommitment records separate
events even when the same lender contributes repeatedly to one request. RequestEvaluation
keeps a single submission observation, its later assessment completion, separate current
checks, and at most one final human decision per request. AssessmentID is the public alias
of the Submission EvaluationID. Current profiles never overwrite frozen request inputs.
Balances, overdue amounts, condition result and reports are derived from retained facts.

### 8. Business Rules and Validation Rules

The final column lists possible enforcement categories as requested by the course guide. It does not select implementation objects or prescribe SQL.

#### 8.1 Invariants and Policies

| ID | Business Rule | Why It Matters | Possible Enforcement Approach |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| BR-01 | Every record has a stable identifier; logins are unique; one user has at most one current BorrowerProfile and one active membership per role. | Prevents ambiguous identity and duplicate memberships. | Primary key, uniqueness, required relationships |
| BR-02 | Each child/event belongs to its stated existing parent; allocations must reference a repayment and installment on the same loan. Relationship cardinalities in Section 7 apply in both directions. | Prevents orphaned and cross-loan records. | Foreign keys, required relationships, controlled validation |
| BR-03 | Active users can perform permitted actions. Restricted users can read their own history, maintain their profile, cancel permitted requests, and repay, but cannot create/edit/submit requests or make new commitments. Inactive users cannot sign in. Deactivation or removal of a Borrower/Lender role is rejected while that user has a Submitted/PendingReview/OpenFunding request, a Pending commitment, or an outstanding loan as borrower or participating lender. Restriction does not void existing obligations or commitments. | Blocks new participation without erasing obligations or payment access. | Entitlement and state validation |
| BR-04 | A Borrower may have multiple Draft, Submitted, PendingReview, and OpenFunding requests and multiple outstanding loans; there is no one-request or one-loan count limit. Submission snapshots include existing platform exposure under BR-07. Concurrent submissions for the same Borrower must be ordered so each later snapshot includes earlier live requests; a request and its resulting loan are never counted twice. | Allows repeat borrowing while assessing combined repayment obligations and preventing concurrent submissions from hiding exposure. | Controlled transaction and concurrency validation |
| BR-05 | Principal must fall within the applicable policy's positive amount range and permitted installment counts. Monetary values use USD cents; values with more than two decimal places are rejected. Required purpose and identity values cannot be blank; income must be positive and existing monthly obligations nonnegative. | Makes obligations and assessment inputs valid and comparable. | Required values, domain/range checks |
| BR-06 | Submission fixes the request terms, income/external-obligation and platform-exposure/delinquency snapshot, submission time, and fixed policy label D1. These cannot be edited afterward. Later profile changes affect future submissions only; D1 remains fixed for this dataset; current platform exposure and delinquency are rechecked for approval/funding under BR-07, with each check retained separately rather than overwriting the original snapshot or assessment. | Preserves the basis of assessment and lender participation. | Finalization rules, immutable version/snapshot protection |
| BR-07 | The demonstration assessment returns Pass only when the proposed largest installment plus the retained combined monthly burden of all other outstanding loans and live requests plus retained external monthly obligations does not exceed retained monthly income, and no outstanding loan was delinquent at the snapshot time. Otherwise return Fail with the failed conditions. The calculation and current approval/funding rechecks follow the assessment detail below. The request must satisfy BR-04 and BR-05; no score, external API, or model is required. | Accounts for multiple obligations and makes delinquency relevant to additional borrowing without imposing a loan-count cap. | Policy evaluation with stored results and reasons |
| BR-08 | Pass leads to PendingReview, not permission to fund. Only an Administrator's approval opens funding. Fail and manual rejection are terminal for that request; a correction requires a new request. An Administrator cannot override an automated Fail. | Separates automated screening from authorization to list a request. | Role and transition validation |
| BR-09 | Every commitment is positive, comes from an active Lender other than the Borrower, and targets an OpenFunding request whose Borrower is active, satisfies the current BR-07 checks, and whose deadline has not arrived. The same Lender may contribute again using a new operation reference; each contribution remains a separate immutable event. Exact retries and conflicting reference reuse follow BR-20. | Allows additional funding while preventing self-funding, duplicate retry effects, and funding of currently ineligible requests. | Event identity and operation-reference uniqueness, ownership, eligibility, and state validation |
| BR-10 | For an open request, the sum of all Pending commitments, including multiple commitments by the same Lender, never exceeds its principal. Each additional commitment cannot exceed the remaining amount. At exact full funding, all Pending commitments become Applied and no further funding is permitted. Concurrent actions preserve this cap and create at most one Loan for this request. | Prevents overfunding and double activation. | Controlled transaction and concurrency validation |
| BR-11 | Cancellation or expiration before activation releases every Pending commitment, records the reason, and creates no Loan. Released commitments remain visible as history and do not count as funded principal. Applied commitments cannot be released. | Defines unsuccessful funding without imaginary refunds. | Atomic status changes and history retention |
| BR-12 | A Loan exists only after full funding; exactly one Loan may arise from a Request. Its principal and installment count are the fixed approved request terms. Commitments are simulated promises, so no wallet balance or transfer is involved. | Separates an application from an obligation to repay. | Unique request-to-loan relationship, transactional validation |
| BR-13 | A Loan has the accepted number of positive installments. All but the last have equal principal rounded down to cents; the last includes the remainder so the sum equals principal. Due dates are monthly anniversaries of activation, starting one month later, using the original activation day or the last day of a shorter month. No interest or fees accrue. | Prevents rounding loss and ambiguous dates. | Schedule validation and dependent-record constraints |
| BR-14 | A new repayment must be posted by the owning Borrower on an Active loan and be greater than zero and no greater than outstanding principal. The system assigns posting time; users cannot backdate or future-date it. | Prevents invented credits, overpayments, and altered delinquency history. | Ownership, range, and state validation |
| BR-15 | Repayments apply only within the explicitly selected owned LoanID, to its oldest unpaid installment first, then later installments, including future ones. Allocations are positive, total exactly the repayment amount, and cannot make an installment's cumulative paid amount exceed its obligation. No amount is automatically moved to another loan of the same Borrower. | Supports partial/early payment without unmatched money. | Allocation validation and atomic transaction |
| BR-16 | Outstanding principal equals original principal minus committed repayments and also equals the sum of unpaid installment amounts. Total principal repaid cannot exceed original principal. Repaid means the outstanding amount is exactly zero. | Provides independently reconcilable balances. | Aggregate reconciliation, completion rule |
| BR-17 | At business date D, an installment is overdue only if its due date is earlier than D and its remaining amount is positive. A loan is Delinquent if any installment is overdue; otherwise it is Current. Overdue principal sums only overdue remainders. Days past due is D minus the oldest unpaid overdue due date. | Distinguishes today's obligation, partial delinquency, and loan completion. | Date/aggregate logic using explicit evaluation time |
| BR-18 | Loan lifecycle (Active/Repaid) and payment condition (Current/Delinquent) are separate and evaluated independently for each loan. Reports and current eligibility checks derive delinquency at their stated time rather than trusting a stale observation. Any outstanding delinquent loan blocks new credit under BR-07; paying one loan does not clear another loan's delinquency. Observations record detection time without moving due dates, and repayment does not automatically remove an Administrator-imposed restriction. | Prevents stale status and inconsistent state meanings. | Derived reporting and controlled observation updates |
| BR-19 | Only the transitions in Section 8.2 are allowed. A valid status code alone does not authorize a transition. Terminal requests and Repaid loans cannot be reopened. | Prevents arbitrary status editing. | Domain checks plus transition/entitlement validation |
| BR-20 | Each funding/repayment submission has a caller-scoped operation reference identifying one intended action. An identical authorized retry returns the original result without another transaction effect or success audit; the same reference with a different action, target, or amount is rejected. A new reference represents a separate action, including an additional contribution by the same Lender to the same request, subject to all current business checks. Equal amounts alone do not make two actions duplicates. | Makes retry behavior explicit without treating coincidentally equal payments as duplicates. | Uniqueness and retry validation |
| BR-21 | Posted repayments, allocations, assessment results, review decisions, and original commitment amounts are immutable. Loan terms and schedules are fixed after activation. A commitment's status and a loan's lifecycle/observation may change only through their permitted operations. | Preserves financial and decision history while allowing normal progression. | Restricted operations, immutability checks |
| BR-22 | Business and audit history is retained for the life of the project dataset. Normal application roles, including Administrator, cannot hard-delete users, referenced records, transactions, fixed D1 configuration, or audit events. Drafts are cancelled rather than deleted. | Preserves traceability and referential integrity. | Restricted privileges, relationship protection |
| BR-23 | Every state-changing action described as atomic includes its required success audit. If any part fails, none of that action's business effects or success events remain. A durable failure/denial event is separate from the rolled-back success transaction. | Prevents partial records and misleading audit claims. | Transaction with commit/rollback; separate failure recording |
| BR-24 | Conflicting submission, approval, funding, cancellation, expiration, and repayment operations are resolved against one valid current state. Borrower-level exposure snapshots and eligibility checks include concurrent requests/loans without double-counting a request-to-loan conversion; request-level checks cover all contributions regardless of Lender. At or after the funding deadline, new funding is rejected even if expiration has not run. Existing commitments remain binding until applied or released. | Protects capacity, deadlines, and replay behavior under concurrency. | Controlled transactions and conflict detection |
| BR-25 | Administrative follow-up is append-only and has exactly one request or loan target. A correction note references an earlier note on the same target. Notes cannot change eligibility decisions, payment amounts, schedules, or balances. | Provides limited, reviewable oversight without financial override powers. | Required relationships and authorized append operation |
| BR-26 | Each report states its row grain, authorized scope, and evaluation time. Count each commitment, installment, loan, or repayment once at that grain even when one Borrower has multiple loans or one Lender has multiple commitments to a loan. Calculate loan-level totals before joining commitment detail; totals and counts cover only records the caller may see. Absence of activity returns zero totals or a labeled empty result. | Prevents multiplication of monetary totals across one-to-many joins. | Query design and independent total checks |

**Combined-exposure assessment detail (BR-04 and BR-07):**

- External monthly obligations are the Borrower's self-reported obligations outside this platform. Platform loans and live requests are added from system records, not entered again in that profile field.
- For each other Active loan, use its largest remaining unpaid installment as its conservative monthly burden. For each other Submitted, PendingReview, or OpenFunding request, use its proposed largest installment; count the full proposed installment even when the request has only partial funding. Draft, Rejected, Cancelled, and Expired requests and Repaid loans contribute zero. When a request becomes Funded, replace its request burden with the resulting loan burden exactly once.
- At submission, retain the combined burden of other live requests/outstanding loans, the no-outstanding-delinquency result, and evaluation time. Do not retain separate historical component identifiers or amounts; current source records are read to calculate the total. Recording a Submitted request makes its proposed burden visible to later submission snapshots; even a not-yet-assessed request counts until it leaves the live states. Concurrent submissions are ordered per Borrower, so they cannot both assess the same unused capacity as if the other request did not exist.
- Assessment uses the retained snapshot: **proposed largest installment + other platform burden + external monthly obligations <= monthly income**, and no outstanding delinquent loan. For example, income of $1,000, external obligations of $100, an existing loan burden of $300, and another live request burden of $200 leave $400 for the proposed installment. Summing largest installments is a conservative classroom policy, not a production credit model.
- Before approval and every new commitment (including final activation), recheck the formula with current platform exposure/delinquency, the target request's retained income/external obligations, and the target request counted once. Retain each check time, combined burden, condition results, and reasons separately. A failed check rejects that approval/commitment without changing the original assessment or earlier commitments; repayment, cancellation, rejection, or expiration can make a later attempt eligible. Existing loans remain repayable, and no loan becomes due early merely because another loan is delinquent. An exact retry of a committed financial action returns its prior authorized result rather than attempting new credit.

#### 9.2 Audit Requirements

Each event includes a stable event identifier, server timestamp, event type, outcome, authenticated actor, acting role, affected business subject and identifier, and operation/context reference. An automated action identifies the service actor; a user-triggered automatic action also retains its initiating user. State changes retain prior and new states. Monetary events reference immutable amounts and allocations so a reviewer can reconstruct the transaction. Sensitive profile changes record the changed field names and protected context rather than exposing raw income, contact values, passwords, or keys in a general audit feed.

| Business Record / Event | Required Additional Context |
| --- | --- |
| User creation, status change, and role grant/revocation | Target user, prior/new status or role membership, Administrator, and reason where required |
| Profile creation/update | Owning user, changed field names, and change time; any retained prior/new sensitive values receive the same protection as the profile |
| Request creation/edit/submission | Owner, changed draft terms, submitted terms, snapshot/evaluation reference including combined-exposure total and delinquency result, and policy label D1 |
| Assessment completion and manual review | Request, outcome, reason codes, assessment or decision reference, reviewer/service actor, and approval-time eligibility-check context without exposing raw private inputs in the general feed |
| Commitment acceptance | CommitmentID, RequestID, Lender, amount, operation reference, current borrower eligibility-check reference, and resulting funding total; each additional contribution is a distinct event |
| Activation | Request and new Loan, every applied commitment, schedule context, eligibility-check reference, and activation time; exposure moves from request to loan without double-counting |
| Cancellation/expiration | Prior/new request state, reason/deadline, and released commitment identifiers |
| Repayment and loan completion | Loan, repayment reference, amount, allocations, before/after principal, and completion state |
| Delinquency change | Loan, prior/new observed condition, evaluation time, oldest unpaid due date, and overdue amount |
| Administrative follow-up | Target request/loan, note identifier, Administrator, and prior note reference for a correction |
| Authorization denial, conflicting operation reference, or observed transaction failure | Attempted action/target, authenticated actor if available, outcome, safe reason, and correlation reference; no private target contents |

Success audit entries commit with their business action. A rolled-back action must not leave a success event. Denied attempts and detected failures are recorded separately after rejection/rollback; they never make an unsuccessful action look committed. A connection killed mid-transaction need not be capable of writing its own failure event. Once recovery can observe an interrupted attempt, its outcome is logged separately, while rollback and inspection establish whether a committed result exists.

The history report must connect a request to its assessment, manual review, commitments, resulting loan, repayments, and significant state changes. Request and loan context must remain traceable even though they have different identifiers. An exact retry may be observed operationally, but creates neither a second financial effect nor a second business-success event.

#### 11.3 Recovery and Completion Boundaries

The interface confirms success only after the business action commits. If the connection is lost and the outcome is unknown, the user retries with the same operation reference or retrieves its recorded result. The system must not create a second financial effect merely because the caller did not receive the first response. A failed assessment may be retried against the same submitted inputs; only one completed result may exist.

Expired or cancelled requests cannot be reopened. Corrected terms require a new request and assessment. A completed loan remains available for reading and audit, but its principal, schedule, and posted transactions remain unchanged. Resetting an isolated sample database through an explicitly requested build is not an application correction workflow.
